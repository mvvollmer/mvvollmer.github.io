Machine Learning Based Identification of Coral Pathogens

Published in Environmental Microbiology (2024), this project used 16S rDNA bacterial gene sequencing data to train machine learning classifiers, including a Multilayer Perceptron, SVM, Random Forest, and Lasso Regression, to predict coral disease states in endangered Caribbean staghorn coral. The best models achieved over 97% classification accuracy. SHAP-based feature importance analysis identified the specific bacterial taxa most predictive of disease, surfacing candidate pathogens for further study.

No-Limit Texas Hold'em Poker Agents

A Multi-Method Approach to Training a Competitive No-Limit Texas Hold'em Poker Agent Using MCTS, CFR, and ReBeL Architectures. Written with Dhananjai Senthil Kumar for Northeastern's Reinforcement Learning course (CS 4180, Chris Amato).

Six-player No-Limit Hold'em is a large, imperfect-information game; opponents' cards are hidden, the state space is continuous, and strategies that work in two-player poker generally do not scale to a table of six. We modeled the problem as a Markov Decision Process and implemented and compared three families of agents: Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS), Counterfactual Regret Minimization (CFR and MCCFR), and a ReBeL-style agent that combines reinforcement learning with regret minimization over public belief states, backed by neural policy, value, and bet-sizing networks.

Across ten trials of 1,000 rounds each, the ReBeL agent consistently outperformed a field of five independent Q-learning agents, building a substantial and steadily widening lead in cumulative chip count. CFR and MCCFR similarly outperformed Q-learning through regret-based strategy refinement, though both struggled to fully capitalize against highly aggressive or adversarial opponents. Against a more diverse agent pool including deterministic all-in and fold strategies, environment-level pot-distribution issues introduced noise into the results, a limitation we documented directly in the paper alongside our findings.

Coral Population Genomics Pipeline

Vollmer Lab, Florida Atlantic University. A two-pass, Snakemake-orchestrated ANGSD population-genomics pipeline built to run identically on a local workstation, SLURM HPC, or AWS EC2.

The pipeline processes 290 whole-genome resequencing samples spanning two Caribbean reef-building coral species, Acropora palmata and A. cervicornis, across three regions (Florida, Panama, and Bonaire). After clone-filtering to 253 unrelated individuals, genotype likelihood analysis in ANGSD retained 2,034,805 SNPs. Downstream analyses include PCA and admixture (PCAngsd), pairwise FST, SMC++ demographic reconstruction, GADMA-based demographic model search, pyrho recombination mapping, and LFMM genotype-environment association for thermal tolerance.

Population structure was unambiguous: at K=2, admixture analysis assigned every sample to one species or the other with zero admixed individuals, and pairwise FST quantified near-complete reproductive isolation (0.43 to 0.46 between species versus 0.05 to 0.11 within species by geography). SMC++ reconstructed independent demographic histories for both species, including a shared bottleneck around 40,000 years ago consistent with Marine Isotope Stage 3. LFMM genotype-environment association identified a single, highly reproducible thermal-tolerance locus at the RNF213 gene, corroborated independently by an introgression (fd) signal roughly 25 times the genome-wide background, suggesting adaptive introgression of heat tolerance between species. While diagnosing a recombination-mapping pipeline stall, I traced the fault to a units mismatch in the pyrho windowing parameter and fixed it, unblocking all downstream recombination and linked-selection analyses.

On the infrastructure side, I engineered an AWS spot-instance cost plan for the compute-heavy stages, cutting per-hour compute cost by roughly 70% versus on-demand pricing, with automated retry, S3 syncing, and auto-stop to bound both cost and failure risk. This work feeds an in-preparation publication.

Systems & other projects

Agentic Automation Framework (HOAO)

A hierarchical multi-agent orchestration framework (Google ADK + Gemini, FastAPI/SSE UI) built across six iterations. Its human-in-the-loop pipeline lets agents draft a Python tool for a task, sandbox-test it with an auto-repair retry loop, and hold it for human approval before caching it for reuse, turning one-off automations into a growing, validated tool library. It includes a telemetry-driven "efficiency maximizer" that routes subtasks to cheaper or more capable models based on difficulty, and a 34-test suite that runs in about one second. Piloted internally at Moviri as a deployed Cloud Run tool under the firm's AI Labs program.
